Threshold Apparatus

An interactive moving-image study of bodily proximity: a sensor reads how close you are to a doorway, and a projected mesh wakes, deforms and settles as you approach, pause and pass.

Everyday life is full of moments between entering and not yet entering: a pause at a doorway, a slower step at a corridor corner, a moment of hesitation before a glass door. They suggest that a spatial boundary is not only a static element, but something perceived and activated by the body’s approach, pause and passage.

The project asks how interactive moving image can make that threshold visible. Projected onto real transitional spaces, the image stops being something to look at and becomes part of the boundary itself: a spatial skin that responds to the body.

      02Research

      In architecture, a threshold is not simply a doorway or an edge but a transitional condition, connecting inside and outside, open and closed, public and private. Its meaning lies less in form than in how it organises action and perception.

      As one approaches a doorway, the body slows; before a transparent boundary, one checks whether entry is possible; in a narrow passage, posture adjusts. Space is not passively used but continuously formed through its relationship with the body.

      So the project should not simply be an interactive video. Moving image acts as a visual skin; bodily distance, movement, pause and orientation are the perceptual data; the image is a threshold field that deforms, expands or contracts; and the physical space is the framework in which meaning is made.

      03Sensor to image

      An Arduino reads distance in real time through an ultrasonic sensor and sends it to Processing over serial. Processing maps the distance to an interaction value that drives mesh displacement, colour, opacity and interference.

      The value is smoothed, so the image responds gradually rather than switching on and off: the threshold reads as a continuous spatial event.

      Scroll through the five captured states

      The five states as captured from the Processing sketch: still while no one is near, waking as a body enters the sensor’s range, peaking at the threshold and settling into a residual trace after they leave.

      04In the doorway

      The installation is projected onto real spaces with transitional qualities, such as corridors, entrance halls and stair landings, and is activated by the body as it approaches. Transience, responsiveness, corporeality and threshold come together in one dynamic boundary.
